
### 3.1 Product Requirements Document (PRD)

Building a good Product Requirements Document (PRD) can take weeks or even months for complex products, as it requires understanding user needs, defining requirements, and documenting the expected behavior of the product.

A PRD typically includes:

- Product goals: What problem are we solving, and why?
- User flows: How users navigate through the product to complete tasks.
- Functional requirements: What features the product must support.
- Non-functional requirements: Expectations around performance, security, reliability, and accessibility.
- Acceptance criteria: How we determine whether a feature works as expected.

Typical workflow: The product team, often working with designers and stakeholders, defines and documents the requirements. The engineering team then uses the PRD to plan, design, implement, and test the features.

Key idea: A PRD aligns everyone on what needs to be built, why it is needed, and how success will be measured before and during development.

Note: Not every PRD takes months to create. The time depends on the product's complexity, scope, and how much discovery is needed. PRDs are also living documents that may evolve as the team learns more.
